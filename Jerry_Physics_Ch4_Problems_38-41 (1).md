# Physics — Chapter 4, Problems 38–41

Textbook: *Physics*, 6th edition, Wilson, Buffa, and Lou.

## Problem 38

### Statement

The engine of a 1.0-kg toy plane exerts a 15-N forward force. If the air exerts an 8.0-N resistive force on the plane, what is the magnitude of the acceleration of the plane?

### Solution

Take forward as positive. Air resistance acts backward, opposite the engine force.

$$F_{\text{net}}=15-8.0=7.0\,\text{N}.$$

Using Newton’s second law:

$$a=\frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m}=\frac{7.0}{1.0}=\boxed{7.0\,\text{m/s}^2\text{ forward}}.$$

Acceleration points in the direction of the net force.

## Problem 39

### Statement

When a horizontal force of 300 N is applied to a 75.0-kg box, the box slides on a level floor, opposed by a force of kinetic friction of 120 N. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of the box?

### Solution

Take the applied force’s direction as positive. Friction acts in the opposite direction.

$$F_{\text{net}}=300-120=180\,\text{N}.$$

$$a=\frac{F_{\text{net}}}{m}=\frac{180}{75.0}=\boxed{2.4\,\text{m/s}^2}.$$

The acceleration is in the direction of the applied force. The upward normal force and downward gravitational force balance, so there is no vertical acceleration.

## Problem 40

### Statement

A rocket is far away from all planets and stars, so gravity is not a consideration. It is using its rocket engines to accelerate upward with an acceleration a = 9.80 m/s². On the floor of the main deck is a crate (object with brick pattern) with a mass of 75.0 kg (Fig. 4.31).

(a) How many forces are acting on the crate: (1) zero; (2) one; (3) two; (4) three?

(b) Determine the normal force on the crate and compare it to the normal force the crate would experience if it were at rest on the surface of the Earth.

### Solution

**(a)** One force acts on the crate: the floor’s upward normal force. **Answer: choice (2).**

Gravity is negligible. The engines push the rocket, and the rocket’s floor pushes the crate. Acceleration is not an additional force.

**(b)** The crate accelerates upward with the rocket. Since the normal force is the only force acting on it:

$$F_N=ma=(75.0)(9.80)=\boxed{735\,\text{N upward}}.$$

At rest on a level surface on Earth, the normal force balances gravity:

$$F_N=mg=(75.0)(9.80)=735\,\text{N}.$$

The normal forces have the same magnitude. In the rocket, the normal force causes upward acceleration; on Earth, it balances the downward gravitational force.

## Problem 41

### Statement

An object (mass 10.0 kg) slides upward on a slippery vertical wall. A force F of 60 N acts at an angle of 60° as shown in Fig. 4.32. (a) Determine the normal force exerted on the object by the wall. (b) Determine the object’s acceleration.

**Diagram description:** The object is against a wall on its left. The applied force points upward and left, into the wall, along a line making 60° with the horizontal.

### Solution

Treat the slippery wall as frictionless. Three forces act on the object: gravity downward, the applied force upward-left, and the wall’s normal force to the right.

**(a) Normal force**

The applied force’s horizontal component has magnitude:

$$|F_x|=F\cos60^\circ=60(0.5)=30\,\text{N}.$$

There is no horizontal acceleration, so the wall balances this leftward component:

$$\boxed{F_N=30\,\text{N to the right}}.$$

**(b) Acceleration**

Take upward as positive. The applied force’s upward component is:

$$F_y=60\sin60^\circ\approx51.96\,\text{N}.$$

The gravitational force is:

$$mg=(10.0)(9.80)=98.0\,\text{N downward}.$$

Apply Newton’s second law vertically:

$$F_y-mg=ma_y.$$

$$a_y=\frac{60\sin60^\circ-(10.0)(9.80)}{10.0}\approx-4.60\,\text{m/s}^2.$$

$$\boxed{a=4.6\,\text{m/s}^2\text{ downward}}.$$

The object moves upward but accelerates downward, so it is slowing down at this instant.

## Key concept: Why “normal” force?

In physics, **normal means perpendicular to a surface**. A normal force is the push a surface exerts on an object touching it.

- A horizontal floor pushes upward.
- A vertical wall pushes horizontally away from the wall.
- A sloped surface pushes perpendicular to the slope.

“Normal” describes the force’s direction. Its magnitude does not always equal mg; use Newton’s second law to determine it.
